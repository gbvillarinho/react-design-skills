# Page copy by type

Structure follows what the reader needs to decide. Use these as starting questions, not fixed templates, and let the real facts decide the order and length.

## Headline and first screen

- Say what it is, for whom, and the main reason to care, using facts. A clever line is optional; a clear one is required.
- The subhead adds the most useful detail the headline left out (what you get, where, when, how much).
- The first button names the outcome: "Order for tomorrow", not "Get started".

## Landing page

| Part | What it answers | Source of content |
|---|---|---|
| Opening | What is this and why now? | The one most distinctive fact |
| Offer | What exactly do I get, and what does it cost? | Real items, prices, options |
| Proof | Why should I believe it? | Numbers, process, real names, dated facts. If you have none, say less instead of inventing |
| How it works | What happens after I act? | Actual steps and times |
| Practical info | Where, when, how do I reach you? | Address, hours, contact |
| Final action | What do I do now? | One clear action, repeating the opening's offer |

Skip any part that has no real content; an empty "Why choose us" section is worse than none.

## Product and pricing pages

- Lead with what the thing does for the buyer, then specs.
- Show price early and say what is included. Avoid "contact us" unless that is truly the model.
- Compare options by differences that matter to a decision, not by feature count.

## About pages

- Facts first: who, since when, where, what changed. One honest detail beats a mission statement.
- Write in the first person plural, or about named people, only if those people exist.

## Testimonials and proof

- Real quotes with a name and context, or none. Never fabricate people, ratings or counts. In a draft, label any sample as a placeholder.

## Length and tone

- Short lines for scanning, but vary their length and shape.
- Match the audience's register. A neighborhood bakery sounds like a person at the counter; a financial tool sounds like a clear colleague.
- Use the locale's natural conventions for dates, currency, and forms of address.

## Final pass

Run [ai-tells.md](ai-tells.md) on everything, then ask for each block: what would the reader miss if this were deleted?

## Text on data and dashboards

- Titles state the finding ("3 stores are behind target"), not the topic.
- Subtitles give period, unit and source in one short line.
- Keep formulas and methodology out of the main view; put them in a hint.
- Write numbers with their unit and comparison ("R$ 169,54, up from R$ 118,40 in September"), in the locale's format.
