# Review checklist

Run this on your own output before finishing. Fix what fails, and mention what you chose not to fix and why.

## Purpose
- [ ] I can state the screen's primary job in one sentence.
- [ ] There is one clearly primary action, and it is the most prominent thing.
- [ ] Nothing on the screen competes with that job without earning its place.

## Layout and hierarchy
- [ ] The eye path matches the order in which the person needs things, and I checked it for the product's reading direction.
- [ ] Groups are visible through space and alignment, not only borders.
- [ ] Destructive actions are separate from the primary action.
- [ ] On mobile, frequent actions are within thumb reach and targets are large enough.

## States
- [ ] Empty, loading, partial, error and success states exist and are designed.
- [ ] Errors say what happened and how to fix it, and keep the person's input.
- [ ] Every action gets visible feedback.
- [ ] Risky actions can be undone, or confirm with a named consequence.

## Forms
- [ ] Every field is necessary, labeled above and always visible.
- [ ] Validation happens at the right moment, with errors next to the fields.
- [ ] The submit button says what it does.

## Language
- [ ] Labels use the person's words, in sentence case and active voice.
- [ ] An action has the same name everywhere in the flow.

## Consistency
- [ ] Similar things look and behave alike across screens.
- [ ] Nothing relies on color alone to carry meaning.

## Reporting
- Summarize in a few lines which heuristics or rules shaped the design, and what you would test with real people.

## Added checks
- [ ] Secondary actions (back, cancel, skip) look secondary and use a conventional pattern, not a second equal button.
- [ ] No component looks oversized or undersized for its role; spacing groups related things.
- [ ] For forms: I questioned which fields are needed now, the step split and the order, and flagged business decisions for confirmation.
